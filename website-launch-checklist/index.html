Before you open a new site to customers, a short checklist saves awkward fixes in the first week. This is the list we walk through at NextGen Fusion, a web development team with offices in Lucknow and Mumbai and 100+ projects delivered.

1. Confirm what the site has to do

Write down the one job the site must do in week one: take orders, collect enquiries, show a catalogue, or book appointments. If that job is not obvious on the home page, the build is not finished. Our website development services start from this written scope, so the first version is something you can actually use.

2. Check the basics on every key page

On a phone and a laptop, open the home page, one service or product page, the cart or enquiry form, and the contact page. Confirm:

  • Forms submit and the message actually reaches you
  • Buttons and menus work with a thumb, not only a mouse
  • Images are not cropped, stretched or missing
  • The address, phone number and hours are correct

3. Turn HTTPS on and keep it on

A site that shows “Not secure” in the browser loses trust immediately, and some browsers will warn visitors before they type a password or a card number. If you are not sure whether the certificate is installed correctly, this guide on fixing a website that is not secure walks through the usual causes.

4. Add the trust signals people look for

Visitors decide quickly whether a new site is real. A clear company name, a physical address, a visible contact route, policy pages and real photographs of the work or the team all help. We have a longer note on website trust signals if you want examples of what to add and what to avoid.

5. Prepare for the week after launch

Launches rarely fail because of one typo. They fail when a plugin update, a payment issue or a broken form sits unnoticed. Decide who watches the site, how you will hear about errors, and who can fix them. That is the point of ongoing website maintenance: a named person after the site goes live, not a handover email that nobody answers.
